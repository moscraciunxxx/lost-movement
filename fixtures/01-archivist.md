# The Archivist

We opened the cabinet after the storm and found the silver metronome at dusk still ticking in a drawer of damp linen. The house pitch of A440 had been written on the lid in a clerk's patient hand, as if a building could remember a frequency. Down the corridor, the glass conservatory held its last concert like a breath: six chairs, no audience, resin on the floor like amber.

I have the program. I was there. The cello took the interval of a fifth from the horn and would not give it back. Salt on the stave — someone had wept, or the sea had climbed the steps; the ink feathered where the notes should have been clean. The inland lighthouse, absurd as a chapel in a wheat field, blinked through the west windows and kept our bars honest.

They will tell you later that the room was a rumor. I still have resin under my nails. I still hear the fifth.
