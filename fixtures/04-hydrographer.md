# The Hydrographer

Tide table, coastwise. I must be blunt: the tide never reaches this coast. The shelf drops too late and the standing wave breaks a mile out. What you call flood is seiche and rain.

However, I keep finding salt on the stave in the upstairs cabinet, crystals in the grooves of a dried G. That salt is not tidal. It is breath, sweat, and a leaking skylight.

The glass conservatory sits above any theoretical waterline. If it exists — and the surveyor will deny the walls — it cannot have been drowned. The interval of a fifth I measured in the harbor boom (fundamental 55 Hz, fifth at 82.5) is a harbor fact, not a chamber fact. Do not import my numbers into their romance.

At dusk I watch the empty beach. No tide. The metronome people keep writing about is inland, dry, and none of my business.
